Privacy policy

Last updated: 9 November 2026

Zuno is made by Christopher Biguet. This policy explains what the app collects, where it is kept and why.

What Zuno collects

  • Your account: email address and password. The password is stored only in scrambled (hashed) form, so nobody can read it, including us. If you use Sign in with Apple, Zuno instead receives from Apple an identifier for your Apple Account and the name and email you choose to share (which can be a private relay address). If you use Google, Zuno receives from Google an identifier for your Google Account, your name and your email address. Zuno never sees your Apple or Google password.
  • Your profile: first name, last name, newsletter choice and, if you add them, a gamertag and a profile photo. Your gamertag is a nickname shown only to you in the app; in this version nobody else can see it.
  • Your learning progress: words studied and learned, saved words and sentences, daily activity and time spent in the app, your level choices, and the streak freezes and hulus you earn or buy.
  • Answers you report: if you tap “I think I was right” in Translate, the sentence and the answer you typed are sent so Zuno can accept more correct answers. They are sent without your name, email or account, and only while Share anonymous usage is on.

Where it is kept

Your account, profile (except the photo) and learning progress are stored in your Zuno account on servers run by Supabase, the service Zuno uses for accounts. That is what lets you sign in on a new iPhone and keeps your progress the same on all your devices. Supabase also records technical details of each sign-in, such as the IP address, to protect your account. Reported answers are kept on Supabase too, apart from any account.

A copy is kept on your iPhone so Zuno works offline. Your profile and sign-in are kept in the iPhone's secure Keychain and are not included in iPhone backups. Your profile photo stays on your iPhone only and is never uploaded.

Account emails (confirming your address, resetting your password, changing your email) are sent through Brevo, which receives your email address for that only. Higher levels are downloaded from Apple's servers when you open them. The Zuno website is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may record your IP address when you visit it.

Why

Your email and password let you sign in and recover your account. Your name or gamertag personalises the app (greetings). Zuno does not ask for your date of birth; a birthday saved by an earlier version stays only to show a birthday message. Your progress powers the lessons and statistics and is synced between your devices. Only if you tick the box, your email will be used for a Zuno newsletter once one exists.

Zuno does not sell your data. It goes only to the services named here, which run parts of the app for Zuno, and is used for nothing beyond running and improving the app.

Analytics and crash reports

Zuno uses TelemetryDeck, a privacy-focused analytics service, to count anonymous usage events, such as a conversation passed or a daily goal reached, together with basic device information (iPhone model, iOS and app version). It never receives your name, email, answers or searches; it uses a random, scrambled (hashed) identifier, so events can't be traced back to you. It is on by default; you can switch it off in Settings › Privacy policy › Share anonymous usage. Apple may share anonymous usage and crash information with the developer if you allowed this in your iPhone's settings.

Speaking practice uses your microphone and Apple's speech recognition on your iPhone only; your voice is never recorded or sent.

Advertising and tracking

This version of Zuno shows no ads and does not track you across other apps or websites.

Purchases

Tips (“Buy me a coffee”) and the Streak pack are processed by Apple. Zuno never sees your payment details; what you buy is added to your Zuno account.

Your choices

In Settings › Profile you can change your details, export all your data as a file, log out, or delete your account. Deleting your account permanently erases your account, profile and progress from Zuno's servers and from this iPhone; for Sign in with Apple it also ends Zuno's access to your Apple Account. Reported answers are not linked to you, so they can't be traced back to you or deleted with your account. Logging out ends the sign-in on this iPhone only.

Children

Zuno is for people aged 13 and over and does not knowingly register younger children.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will appear here. Questions: support@zunolanguages.com.
